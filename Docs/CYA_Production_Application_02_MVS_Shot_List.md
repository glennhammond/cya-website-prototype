# CYA Production Application 02 — Minimum Viable Shoot Field Shot List

**Status:** FIELD-READY  
**Decision:** Option B — Minimum Viable Shoot  
**Creative authority:** **Workday, Interrupted**  
**Behaviour:** **WORK CONTEXT → NOTICE → USEFUL ACTION → RETURN**  
**Production model:** one genuine Brisbane workplace · one working day · still + motion

## 1. Shoot objective

Create a compact, reusable CYA image and motion library that feels unmistakably grounded in working life. The priority is not to record many different wellbeing activities. The priority is to capture a small number of believable workday moments extremely well, with enough crop and format flexibility for website, social, client activation, email and future campaign use.

Every priority setup should preserve ordinary work context before, during and after the wellbeing moment.

## 2. Priority hierarchy

### P0 — Must leave the shoot with these

1. Homepage four-beat hero sequence: **work → notice → useful action → return**.
2. Strong landscape and portrait hero stills from the same sequence.
3. Human Signal detail library with clear workplace evidence.
4. Genuine hybrid-work sequence with in-room and remote participation visible together.
5. Varied-participation group sequence — people making different proportionate choices rather than synchronised movement.
6. Return-to-work imagery: typing, writing, discussion, walking back into the day.

### P1 — Capture once P0 is secure

7. Workshop / Lunch & Learn interaction.
8. Meeting-room listening and note-taking.
9. Circulation and transition moments between spaces.
10. Facilitator-in-context frames that show responsiveness rather than performance.

### P2 — Nice additions

11. Workplace establishing details.
12. Empty-room / environmental frames for design use.
13. Extra vertical material for social stories/reels.
14. Additional hands, objects and desk details.

---

## 3. Sequence A — Homepage hero / primary Workday Window

**Purpose:** strongest expression of Workday, Interrupted.  
**People:** 1–2 workers; facilitator optional.  
**Location:** desk area or meeting room with unmistakable work context.  
**Target time:** 75–90 minutes.

### A1 — Work context

Capture the person genuinely:

- typing at a laptop;
- reading/reviewing a document;
- taking notes;
- listening to a colleague;
- participating in an ordinary meeting.

**Stills required**

- landscape wide, subject right with negative space left;
- landscape wide, subject left with negative space right;
- landscape medium;
- portrait 4:5;
- square-safe frame;
- dedicated 9:16-safe frame.

**Motion required**

- 8–12 sec locked or restrained shot of ordinary work;
- 8–12 sec closer detail of hands/screen/notebook;
- one 9:16 version.

### A2 — Notice

Small, credible human signal — never exaggerated stress.

Capture:

- shoulders settling or shifting;
- hand briefly leaving mouse/keyboard;
- wrist roll;
- eyes leaving the screen for a moment;
- breath/pause;
- slight chair adjustment;
- hand briefly touching shoulder/neck without acting pain.

**Priority:** medium and detail framing with work context still legible.

### A3 — Useful action

Keep action proportionate to the room and workday.

Capture alternatives:

- seated shoulder mobility;
- seated twist;
- wrist/forearm release;
- standing shoulder roll beside desk;
- brief breathing cue;
- posture reset;
- facilitator demonstrates while worker adapts naturally.

Do **not** line people up or turn this into a yoga class.

### A4 — Return

This is essential and easy to forget.

Capture:

- hands return to keyboard;
- notebook reopens;
- eyes return to screen;
- colleague resumes conversation;
- person walks back into meeting room;
- worker settles back into task.

### A5 — Continuous motion sequence

Capture one clean **15–25 second** sequence containing all four beats:

1. working;
2. noticing;
3. brief useful action;
4. return to work.

Shoot once landscape and, if time permits, once as a deliberately composed vertical take.

---

## 4. Sequence B — Human Signal library

**Purpose:** flexible distinctive details for Home, Programs, Workshops, campaigns and client activation.  
**Target time:** 45–60 minutes across the day rather than one staged block.

Capture at least 15–20 usable details:

1. hand leaving mouse;
2. fingers resting on keyboard before a pause;
3. wrist roll beside laptop;
4. shoulder shift in chair;
5. hand on notebook while listening;
6. pen moving during workshop;
7. face listening, not posing;
8. eyes moving from screen to colleague;
9. headphones removed or adjusted;
10. phone placed face-down beside work materials;
11. ordinary coffee/tea/water beside notebook;
12. feet repositioning under desk;
13. chair adjustment;
14. hands resting briefly after typing;
15. person standing from desk;
16. hand opening meeting-room door;
17. walking through corridor;
18. laptop and notebook carried between spaces;
19. remote colleague visible over an in-room participant's shoulder;
20. return to typing/writing after the interruption.

**Rule:** every detail must still contain enough workplace evidence to avoid becoming an anonymous wellness stock image.

For the best 6–8 details, capture both landscape and vertical versions.

---

## 5. Sequence C — Genuine hybrid work

**Purpose:** fill one of the current website's biggest structural image gaps.  
**People:** 3–4 in room + 1 real/consenting remote participant.  
**Location:** normal meeting room with ordinary AV screen.  
**Target time:** 60–75 minutes.

### Required shots

1. normal hybrid meeting before wellbeing appears;
2. remote participant clearly visible while in-room people remain primary;
3. facilitator introduces a brief reset;
4. one in-room person remains seated;
5. one person stands;
6. one person adapts or simply listens;
7. remote participant follows at their own level;
8. close Human Signal details during the pause;
9. return to meeting/discussion;
10. remote participant remains included in the return frame.

### Technical precautions

- use dummy/non-confidential screen content;
- avoid visible personal names, notifications or client data;
- expose screen and faces carefully;
- capture a clean wide landscape that keeps both room and remote participant readable;
- capture a dedicated vertical composition rather than relying only on cropping the wide.

---

## 6. Sequence D — Participation without performance

**Purpose:** show accessibility, choice and workplace fit.  
**People:** 4–6.  
**Location:** desk / meeting / breakout area.  
**Target time:** 45–60 minutes.

Capture a moment where people are intentionally **not identical**:

- one seated;
- one standing;
- one using a smaller range of movement;
- one observing/listening;
- facilitator demonstrating rather than physically correcting participants;
- ordinary chairs, laptops, notebooks and work surfaces remain visible.

### Must-have frames

- wide landscape group;
- medium two-person interaction;
- facilitator with one participant in context;
- portrait participant adaptation;
- vertical group variation;
- end frame with people returning to chairs/screens/conversation.

---

## 7. Sequence E — Workshop / Lunch & Learn

**Purpose:** strengthen Workshops and future client-activation material. Existing imagery already works, so this is secondary to P0 coverage.  
**People:** facilitator/expert + 4–8 participants.  
**Target time:** 45–60 minutes.

Capture:

1. facilitator speaking with participants visible;
2. participant listening;
3. participant asking a real/simple question;
4. hands taking notes;
5. participant referring to takeaway/resource;
6. two colleagues exchanging a brief comment;
7. facilitator listening to participant rather than always presenting;
8. wide room context;
9. medium conversational frame;
10. close Human Signal details;
11. end-of-session transition;
12. people returning to ordinary work.

Avoid podium-style distance, staged applause, forced laughter and unreadable projection screens.

---

## 8. Sequence F — Transitions and return-to-work

**Purpose:** make the creative idea believable beyond the wellbeing activity itself.  
**Target time:** collect throughout day + 30 dedicated minutes.

Capture:

- leaving desk for meeting room;
- walking corridor with laptop/notebook;
- waiting briefly at doorway;
- entering breakout area;
- sitting back down at desk;
- reopening laptop;
- resuming conversation;
- putting headphones back on;
- returning to keyboard;
- group dispersing naturally after workshop;
- facilitator leaving while the workplace continues.

These frames are especially valuable for editorial sequences, web transitions and motion edits.

---

## 9. Environmental / design utility frames

Capture only after the people-based P0 material is secure.

- clean desk environment with ordinary work objects;
- meeting room before people arrive;
- corridor / doorway / glass partition;
- laptop + notebook with clean copy-safe space;
- workplace signage only if permission allows it;
- wide room frame with generous negative space;
- texture/details that unmistakably read as working life.

Do not manufacture a wellness set with plants, mats, candles or activewear props.

---

## 10. Crop checklist for every P0 setup

Before leaving each major scene, confirm:

- [ ] landscape 3:2 master;
- [ ] wide 16:9-safe composition;
- [ ] subject-left copy-safe variant;
- [ ] subject-right copy-safe variant;
- [ ] portrait 4:5;
- [ ] square-safe crop latitude;
- [ ] dedicated 9:16 composition;
- [ ] close Human Signal detail;
- [ ] return-to-work frame;
- [ ] 6–12 sec landscape motion clip;
- [ ] 6–12 sec vertical motion clip for P0 scenes.

---

## 11. Lean capture guidance

If the production is being run very lean, protect the shoot in this order:

1. secure A1–A5 completely before chasing variety;
2. use the same desk/meeting setup to collect Human Signals;
3. move to Hybrid Sequence C;
4. capture varied-participation Sequence D;
5. collect workshop material only after the first four are safe;
6. record transitions and return frames continuously during resets/location moves.

Do not burn time repeatedly changing wardrobe, props or locations. The library becomes distinctive through **observation, sequencing and working-life truth**, not through the number of setups.

---

## 12. End-of-day minimum coverage gate

Do not wrap until there is at least:

- [ ] 3 strong Homepage landscape hero still candidates;
- [ ] 3 strong Homepage portrait/vertical candidates;
- [ ] 1 complete landscape four-beat motion sequence;
- [ ] 1 usable vertical four-beat or modular sequence;
- [ ] 15+ Human Signal details;
- [ ] 5+ genuine hybrid-work stills;
- [ ] 3+ hybrid motion clips;
- [ ] 5+ varied-participation stills;
- [ ] 5+ clear return-to-work stills;
- [ ] 5+ return/transition motion clips;
- [ ] enough clean workplace context to make every wellbeing frame believable.

## 13. Avoid list — final reminder

Do not capture or select:

- generic yoga-studio imagery;
- rows of synchronised stretching unless the real service genuinely requires it;
- staged laughter;
- exaggerated stress/pain acting;
- wellness wardrobe replacing normal workplace clothing;
- candles/incense/artificial wellness props;
- difficult poses or flexibility demonstrations;
- facilitator physically arranging bodies for visual symmetry;
- confidential screens, whiteboards or documents;
- imagery where the return to work feels implausible.

The test for every final image is simple:

**Could this only happen inside a real working day — and does the useful interruption feel proportionate to that day?**
